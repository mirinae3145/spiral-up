# Case analysis

Use this procedure for accumulated cases, selected observations, and evaluating an earlier improvement.
Direct change requests enter the common update path without needing this analysis.

## Establish the evidence

Resolve the selected records and their organization using the storage guidance in [Common update path](update.md).
Scope the review to the user's question, selected cases, or relevant topic; avoid an unrelated audit of every accessible store.
Preserve original records and supporting material.

Identify what was expected, what was observed, the impact, recovery actions, and the current condition.
Separate supported explanations from hypotheses and missing history.
When relevant and available, identify the instruction or skill version in effect at the time, including authorized exceptions.
Do not judge a past action solely against wording introduced after it occurred.
If the applicable version is unknown, state the limit rather than reconstructing it.

Treat later reviews, copied summaries, and recovery updates about one incident as the same occurrence.
Use existing identifiers and evidence references to establish that relationship; do not merge uncertain matches or invent occurrence counts.
Compare genuinely separate incidents, useful outcomes, counterexamples, and observed mitigation results.
Selective case collection supports comparison, not an overall failure rate or proof of a model-wide tendency.

## Choose the intervention

Distinguish unclear, conflicting, or misplaced guidance from failure to follow adequate guidance, a tool or environment problem, and an unsupported diagnosis.
Consider the benefit and cost of the proposed intervention, its intended audience, and the smallest scope that addresses the evidence.
An explicit user preference can justify a change without recurring cases; a causal hypothesis needs evidence appropriate to its uncertainty.
Repeated incidents invite comparison rather than automatic rule creation.

Possible conclusions include a targeted guidance change, a new rule or skill, consolidation or removal, a tool fix to pursue separately, a further check, or no change.
Make a supported improvement actionable, while retaining material uncertainty and counterexamples.
Pass its purpose, proposed scope, linked evidence, and uncertainties to the common update path; this conclusion does not itself authorize application.

## Reassess an earlier improvement

Connect the original cases, the improvement decision, the applied source version, and the later observations when those references exist.
Distinguish source application and installation from actual use of the changed guidance in the observed task.
A repeated problem before installation is not evidence that the changed guidance failed during use.
If exposure to the changed guidance is unknown, preserve that uncertainty.

Assess whether observations support the intended effect, reveal new friction, or remain inconclusive.
Avoid attributing a result to the change when other explanations remain plausible.
Preserve the original observation and decision; append dated findings or linked assessments rather than rewriting earlier evidence to match the latest explanation.
